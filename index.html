📄 The History of Tea

ĐOẠN A

Tea is one of the most widely consumed drinks in the world today, second only to water. It is enjoyed in countless forms across nearly every culture, from the formal tea ceremonies of Japan to the casual afternoon cup in British homes. But few people realise that this everyday beverage has a rich and complex past that spans thousands of years and several continents.

ĐOẠN B

According to popular legend, tea was discovered by accident around 2737 BCE when the Chinese Emperor Shen Nung was resting beneath a tree. Some leaves from the tree fell into his pot of boiling water, creating a pleasant aroma. Curious, the emperor tasted the liquid and found it refreshing. Although this story cannot be verified, historical records confirm that tea cultivation began in China and gradually spread to neighbouring countries.

ĐOẠN C

By the 17th century, tea had reached Europe through Dutch and Portuguese traders. At first, it was an expensive luxury that only the wealthy could afford. However, as trade routes expanded and the British East India Company increased imports, the price fell dramatically. Tea soon became a staple in households across Britain, giving rise to traditions such as afternoon tea in the 19th century.

ĐOẠN D

Today, tea is grown in over 40 countries, with China, India, Kenya and Sri Lanka among the largest producers. Advances in technology have transformed production methods, making tea more accessible than ever before. Yet despite these changes, many traditional customs surrounding tea remain unchanged, connecting modern drinkers with centuries of history.
